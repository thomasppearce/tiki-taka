# The defensive line and the offside trap

A team's defensive line is the height at which its back four hold position without the ball. A high line keeps the team compact and squeezes the space the opponent can play in. A deep line protects the space behind but invites pressure.

## The offside trap

Defenders who step up together at the moment a pass is played can leave an attacker in an offside position. Done well, it kills attacks without a tackle. Done badly, a single defender who fails to step up plays everyone onside, and a runner is through on goal.

## Runs in behind

Attackers beat a high line by timing runs: holding just onside until the pass, then sprinting into the space behind. A run released a moment late is offside; repeated offsides against one team usually mean their forwards are being caught by a well-drilled line, or are going too early.

## Reading it on air

A high line plus several offsides suggests the trap is working. A goal from a ball over the top suggests the line has finally been beaten.
